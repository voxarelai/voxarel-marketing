Glossary

What is cargo consolidation?

Cargo consolidation is the practice of combining several smaller shipments from different shippers into one larger, fuller load, so they share the cost and space of a single container or flight, then get separated again at the destination.

How cargo consolidation works

A consolidator collects individual LCL (less-than-container-load) shipments heading to the same region, groups them into one FCL (full-container-load) movement or a single air consolidation, and ships them together under one master document. At the destination, the load is de-consolidated and each shipment is released to its receiver under its own house document.

Why it matters

Consolidation is how forwarders turn many small, unprofitable shipments into one economical movement. Shippers get near-FCL rates on small volumes; the consolidator earns the margin between the buy rate for the full container and the sell rate charged to each shipper. The hard part is the accounting: splitting cost, weight, volume and cash back to each customer accurately.

A worked example

Five shippers each send 0.4 CBM from Dubai to Chennai. Individually each pays an LCL minimum; consolidated into one 2 CBM movement, they share a single sea freight cost plus destination handling, and each is billed their pro-rata share. Cargo consolidation FAQ

What is the difference between consolidation and groupage?

They mean the same thing in most markets: combining multiple shippers' goods into one movement. “Groupage” is the term used more in Europe and the Gulf; “consolidation” is more common elsewhere.

Is consolidation cheaper than shipping separately?

Usually yes for small volumes. Shippers share the cost of a full container or air consolidation, so each pays less than an individual LCL minimum, at the cost of a little extra transit and handling time.

How is cost split between shippers?

By chargeable weight or volume, whichever is greater, plus shared destination and handling fees. Voxarel calculates and reconciles each share automatically.
